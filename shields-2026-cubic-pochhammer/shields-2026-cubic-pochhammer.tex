\begin{abstract}
Multiple-Pochhammer power series associated with a nonnegative log-concave sequence with no internal zeros
were shown by Karp and Zhang to be coefficientwise log-concave at multiplicity two,
while the cubic case remained open.  We prove the cubic conjecture, in the equivalent
fixed-sum form of coefficientwise Schur-concavity.  Together with a degree-three
obstruction for every larger multiplicity, this yields the sharp classification:
universal coefficientwise log-concavity holds precisely at multiplicities two and
three.  Degreewise, the coefficient inequalities are equivalent to monotonicity of a
symmetric residue kernel weighted by the anti-diagonal products of the sequence, while
log-concavity with no internal zeros is exactly central increase of these weights in
every degree.  At multiplicity three we prove this kernel monotonicity for every
nonnegative centrally increasing symmetric weight sequence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

For an integer \(r\ge2\), consider the modified multiple-Pochhammer series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-r}
 F_{f,r}(\mu;x)
 =\sum_{n\ge1}f_n\frac{(\mu)_{rn}}{(rn-1)!}x^n,
\end{equation}
where \((f_n)\) is a nonnegative log-concave sequence with no internal zeros.  Karp
and Zhang proved coefficientwise log-concavity for \(r=2\)
\cite[Thm.~2]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}, gave numerical counterexamples for
\(r\ge4\) \cite[Rem.~5]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}, and formulated the cubic case
\(r=3\) as their Conjecture~1.  Karp and Zhang report that A.~Ninh suggested the construction
to them and conjectured log-concavity for the resulting family
\cite[\S3]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}; related results on log-concavity and
Tur\'an-type inequalities appear in
\cite{KalmykovKarp2013Reciprocal,KalmykovKarp2013Ratios,KarpSitnik2010LogConvexity,NinhPham2025LogConcavity}.

A parameter-dependent formal power series \(F(\mu;x)\) is
\emph{coefficientwise log-concave} if, for every \(\mu,\alpha,\beta\ge0\), all
coefficients of
\[
 F(\mu+\alpha;x)F(\mu+\beta;x)
 -F(\mu;x)F(\mu+\alpha+\beta;x)
\]
are nonnegative.  This is stronger than pointwise log-concavity wherever the series
converges.  Here ``log-concave with no internal zeros'' means that
\(f_n^2\ge f_{n-1}f_{n+1}\) wherever defined and that the positive support is an
interval of integers.  These are Karp and Zhang's hypotheses
\cite[Def.~1]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}; the interval-support condition is essential
(\Cref{rem:internal-zeros}).  Except in \Cref{cor:ordinary}, the arguments are
formal and require no growth assumption.  We use \((\mu)_j\) for the rising
factorial.

\subsection{Main results}\label{subsec:intro-main}

We prove the missing cubic case in an equivalent, base-point-free form.  A
symmetric function of two variables is Schur-concave
\cite[Def.~3.A.1]{MarshallOlkinArnold2011Majorization} exactly when, at each fixed
sum, it decreases as the two variables move apart.

\begin{theorem}[Cubic coefficientwise Schur-concavity]\label{thm:main}
Let \((f_n)_{n\ge1}\) be a nonnegative log-concave sequence with no internal
zeros, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-def}
 F_f(\mu;x)=\sum_{n\ge1}f_n\frac{(\mu)_{3n}}{(3n-1)!}x^n.
\end{equation}
For every \(s>0\) and every \(0\le d_1\le d_2\le s/2\), the formal power series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-def}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal S_f(s;d_1,d_2;x)
 &=F_f\left(\tfrac s2+d_1;x\right)F_f\left(\tfrac s2-d_1;x\right)\\
 &\quad-F_f\left(\tfrac s2+d_2;x\right)F_f\left(\tfrac s2-d_2;x\right)
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
has nonnegative coefficients.  Equivalently, for every \(\mu,\alpha,\beta\ge0\),
the generalized Tur\'anian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Turan-def}
 \Tur_f(\mu;\alpha,\beta;x)
 =F_f(\mu+\alpha;x)F_f(\mu+\beta;x)
 -F_f(\mu;x)F_f(\mu+\alpha+\beta;x)
\end{equation}
has nonnegative coefficients.
\end{theorem}

The fixed-sum statement is equivalent to the generalized Tur\'anian formulation.  A
triple \((\mu,\alpha,\beta)\) corresponds to
\[
 s=2\mu+\alpha+\beta,\qquad
 d_1=\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2},\qquad
 d_2=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2},
\]
and conversely
\[
 \mu=\frac s2-d_2,\qquad
 \alpha=d_2+d_1,\qquad
 \beta=d_2-d_1.
\]
The inverse correspondence is determined up to the symmetry
\(\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\).  Thus the two families of coefficient inequalities
are identical, including the boundary case \(\mu=0\).

Together with the quadratic theorem of Karp and Zhang, the cubic result yields the
sharp multiplicity classification: universal coefficientwise log-concavity holds
exactly for \(r=2,3\) (\Cref{cor:multiplicity}).  For every \(r\ge4\), failure already
occurs in degree three for a two-term log-concave sequence
(\Cref{prop:multiplicity-threshold}).

\subsection{Central-slope cancellation at multiplicity three}\label{subsec:intro-central-slope}

The threshold is not accidental.  At degree three, the derivative at the center of
the constant-weight residue kernel contains the factor \(r(3-r)\); see
\eqref{eq:r-central-slope}.  The quadratic case has ordinary central curvature;
for every \(r\ge4\), the curvature reverses sign and gives an obstruction.  At
\(r=3\) the quadratic term vanishes exactly: the center is a quartically flat
maximum.  The main work of the paper is to show that this critical local cancellation
extends to global kernel monotonicity in every degree and for every admissible weight
sequence.

\subsection{Proof strategy and organization}\label{subsec:intro-organization}

The proof aligns one notion of centrality at three levels.  On the sequence side,
log-concavity with interval support is equivalent to central increase of every
anti-diagonal product sequence \(f_kf_{m-k}\) (\Cref{lem:central-products}).  On the
parameter side, a beta-binomial representation turns the degree-\(m\) coefficient
into an expectation of a symmetric residue kernel in the coordinate
\(q=p(1-p)\); increasing parameter imbalance moves this coordinate away from the
center in likelihood-ratio order (\Cref{lem:beta-order}).  The cubic-specific input
is therefore the kernel theorem \Cref{thm:kernel}: centrally increasing symmetric
weights produce a kernel increasing toward \(p=1/2\).  Beta concentration gives the
converse implication, so the kernel condition is an exact reduction rather than only
a sufficient device (\Cref{prop:kernel-exact}).

\Cref{sec:reduction} establishes this exact reduction.  \Cref{sec:kernel} proves the
cubic kernel theorem, first for constant weights and then for the full monotone-weight
cone.  \Cref{sec:cubic-proof} assembles the proof of \Cref{thm:main} and determines
its strict coefficient support.  \Cref{sec:threshold} proves the sharp multiplicity
threshold, and \Cref{sec:consequences} records analytic and hypergeometric
consequences.  Finally, \Cref{sec:conclusion} summarizes what remains beyond the
universal threshold.

\section{Exact reduction to a residue kernel}\label{sec:reduction}

The sequence condition and the parameter comparison can both be converted into
centrality statements.  On the sequence side, Levit and Mandrescu
\cite[Lem.~2.2(ii)]{LevitMandrescu2006LogConcavity} give
a cross-product characterization of nonnegative log-concave functions without
internal zeros; the following adjacent anti-diagonal form is the interface needed
here.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:central-products}
Let \((f_n)_{n\ge1}\) be a nonzero nonnegative sequence.  The following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \((f_n)\) is log-concave and has no internal zeros.
\item For every \(m\ge2\), the anti-diagonal products are nondecreasing toward
the center:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-products}
 f_kf_{m-k}\le f_{k+1}f_{m-k-1}
 \qquad(1\le k<\lfloor m/2\rfloor).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that \(f\) is log-concave with interval support.  If
\(f_kf_{m-k}=0\), \eqref{eq:central-products} is immediate.  Otherwise interval
support gives \(f_j>0\) for \(k\le j\le m-k\).  With
\(\rho_j=f_{j+1}/f_j\), log-concavity makes \((\rho_j)\) nonincreasing, and
\(k\le m-k-1\) gives
\[
 \frac{f_{k+1}f_{m-k-1}}{f_kf_{m-k}}
 =\frac{\rho_k}{\rho_{m-k-1}}\ge1.
\]

Conversely, assume \eqref{eq:central-products} for every \(m\).  Taking
\(m=2j\) and \(k=j-1\) gives \(f_j^2\ge f_{j-1}f_{j+1}\),
so \(f\) is log-concave.  If \(f_a,f_b>0\) with \(b\ge a+2\), take
\(m=a+b\) and \(k=a\).  Then
\[
 f_{a+1}f_{b-1}\ge f_af_b>0.
\]
Thus both inward neighbors are positive; iteration fills every gap between two
positive terms.  Hence the positive support is an interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:internal-zeros}
The central-product characterization also explains why interval support is
essential.  A gap in the positive support forces some anti-diagonal product
sequence outside the centrally nondecreasing cone: if \(f_a,f_b>0\) but an
intermediate term vanishes, the inward inequality used in the converse of
\Cref{lem:central-products} must fail somewhere.  This is not merely a
limitation of the proof.  Take \(f_1=f_4=1\) and \(f_n=0\) otherwise.  The
log-concavity inequalities still hold, but the support \(\{1,4\}\) is not an
interval.  At \(m=5\) the anti-diagonal products are \(f_1f_4=f_4f_1=1\) and
\(f_2f_3=f_3f_2=0\), so they decrease toward the center, and
\[
 [x^5]\Tur_f(2;1,1;x)=-9360.
\]
Thus failure of sequence centrality can produce an actual sign reversal.
\end{remark}

We now pass from anti-diagonal weights to the parameter comparison, keeping the
weights arbitrary for the moment.  Fix \(m\ge2\) and nonnegative weights
\((w_k)_{1\le k\le m-1}\).  For \(u,v\ge0\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-def}
 C_{m,w}(u,v)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}w_k
 \frac{(u)_{3k}(v)_{3(m-k)}}{(3k-1)![3(m-k)-1]!}.
\end{equation}
Both \(C_{m,w}(0,v)\) and \(C_{m,w}(u,0)\) vanish.  For \(u,v>0\), put
\(N=3m\) and let \(J\) have the beta-binomial law
\[
 \Pp(J=j)=\binom Nj\frac{(u)_j(v)_{N-j}}{(u+v)_N},
 \qquad 0\le j\le N.
\]
Since
\[
 \frac{(u)_j(v)_{N-j}}{(j-1)!(N-j-1)!}
 =\frac{(u+v)_N}{N!}\,j(N-j)\Pp(J=j),
\]
defining
\[
 \widetilde w_j=
 \begin{cases}
  w_{j/3},&0<j<N\ \text{and}\ 3\mid j,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-beta-binomial}
 C_{m,w}(u,v)
 =\frac{(u+v)_N}{N!}\E\!\left[J(N-J)\widetilde w_J\right].
\end{equation}
The endpoint terms vanish because of the factor \(J(N-J)\).

Using the standard mixture \(P\sim\operatorname{Beta}(u,v)\),
\(J\mid P\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,P)\), together with
\[
 j(N-j)\binom Nj=N(N-1)\binom{N-2}{j-1},
\]
we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-beta}
 C_{m,w}(u,v)
 =\frac{(u+v)_N}{N!}N(N-1)\E\,G_{m,w}(P),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G-weighted}
 G_{m,w}(p)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}w_k\binom{3m-2}{3k-1}
 p^{3k}(1-p)^{3(m-k)},
 \qquad 0\le p\le1.
\end{equation}
The shift from \(3k\) to \(3k-1\) comes from
\(j-1\equiv2\pmod3\).  If \(w_k=w_{m-k}\), then
\(G_{m,w}(p)=G_{m,w}(1-p)\).  In that case write
\(\widehat G_{m,w}(q)\) for the common value of \(G_{m,w}(p)\) at the two points
satisfying \(p(1-p)=q\), \(0\le q\le1/4\).  For fixed \(s>0\), let
\[
 P_d\sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right),
 \qquad Q_d=P_d(1-P_d).
\]
Then \eqref{eq:C-beta} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-sum}
 C_{m,w}\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right)
 =\kappa_{m,s}\,\E\,\widehat G_{m,w}(Q_d),
 \qquad
 \kappa_{m,s}=\frac{(s)_{3m}}{(3m)!}\,3m(3m-1)>0.
\end{equation}
Thus \(q=p(1-p)\) is the common centrality coordinate for the residue kernel and
the beta mixture: kernel monotonicity toward \(p=1/2\) is exactly monotonicity
of \(\widehat G_{m,w}\) in \(q\), while \Cref{lem:beta-order} will show that
parameter imbalance moves \(Q_d\) in the opposite direction.



At fixed parameter sum, imbalance is ordered monotonically by the beta mixture.  The
comparison is a likelihood-ratio order; see
\cite[\S1.C]{ShakedShanthikumar2007StochasticOrders}.  It compares the symmetric
transforms \(P(1-P)\); since \(p\mapsto p(1-p)\) increases on \([0,1/2]\), this
is the folded-beta comparison of
\cite[Thm.~3]{BerenhautBergen2011StochasticOrderings}, whose hypotheses reduce at a
fixed parameter sum to
\(2d_1,2d_2\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)\).  The direct proof below removes that
restriction, giving the comparison for every \(0\le d_1<d_2<s/2\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:beta-order}
Fix \(s>0\).  For \(0\le d<s/2\), let
\(P_d\sim\operatorname{Beta}(s/2+d,\,s/2-d)\) and \(Q_d=P_d(1-P_d)\).  If
\(0\le d_1<d_2<s/2\), then \(Q_{d_2}\le_{\mathrm{lr}}Q_{d_1}\), where
\(X\le_{\mathrm{lr}}Y\) means that the ratio of the density of \(Y\) to the
density of \(X\) is nondecreasing on their common support.
Consequently, if \(H:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\) is symmetric, \(H(p)=H(1-p)\), and nondecreasing on
\([0,1/2]\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-order}
 d_1\le d_2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \E H(P_{d_1})\ge \E H(P_{d_2}).
\end{equation}
If \(H\) is strictly increasing on \((0,1/2)\) and \(d_1<d_2\), then the inequality
is strict.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Such an \(H\) is bounded and Borel measurable, so the expectations in
\eqref{eq:beta-order} exist.  The case \(d_1=d_2\) is immediate, so assume
\(d_1<d_2\).  Here
\(Q_d\in(0,1/4)\).  For
\[
 p_\pm(q)=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4q}}{2},
 \qquad
 \ell(q)=\log\frac{p_+(q)}{p_-(q)},
\]
the two preimages of \(q\) satisfy \(p_+p_-=q\) and \(p_-=1-p_+\), so they contribute
\(p_+^{s/2+d-1}p_-^{s/2-d-1}+p_-^{s/2+d-1}p_+^{s/2-d-1}
 =2q^{s/2-1}\cosh\bigl(d\ell(q)\bigr)\).  Since
\(|\mathrm dq/\mathrm dp|=\sqrt{1-4q}\), the density of \(Q_d\) is
\[
 g_d(q)=\frac{2}{B(s/2+d,s/2-d)}\,
 q^{s/2-1}(1-4q)^{-1/2}\cosh\!\bigl(d\ell(q)\bigr).
\]
The densities \(g_{d_1}\) and \(g_{d_2}\) share the factor
\(q^{s/2-1}(1-4q)^{-1/2}\), so
\[
 \frac{g_{d_2}(q)}{g_{d_1}(q)}
 =\frac{B(s/2+d_1,s/2-d_1)}{B(s/2+d_2,s/2-d_2)}\,
  \frac{\cosh(d_2\ell(q))}{\cosh(d_1\ell(q))}.
\]
The function \(\ell\) is strictly decreasing, since \(p_+(q)/p_-(q)\) decreases to
\(1\) as \(q\uparrow1/4\).  For \(d_2>d_1\), \(\cosh(d_2\ell)/\cosh(d_1\ell)\) is
strictly increasing in \(\ell>0\), because \(d\mapsto d\tanh(d\ell)\) is strictly
increasing; hence \(g_{d_2}/g_{d_1}\) is strictly decreasing in \(q\).  Thus \(Q_{d_2}\le_{\mathrm{lr}}Q_{d_1}\).  Since both densities integrate to one,
the strictly monotone ratio crosses \(1\) exactly once; hence
\(g_{d_1}-g_{d_2}\) changes sign once, from negative to positive, and for every
nondecreasing \(\phi\),
\[
 \E\phi(Q_{d_1})-\E\phi(Q_{d_2})
 =\int_0^{1/4}\bigl(\phi(q)-\phi(q^\ast)\bigr)
   \bigl(g_{d_1}(q)-g_{d_2}(q)\bigr)\,\mathrm d q\ge0,
\]
where \(q^\ast\) is the crossing point and the integrand is nonnegative
throughout.  Finally, symmetry of \(H\) gives \(H(p)=\widetilde H(p(1-p))\), with
\(\widetilde H\) nondecreasing on \([0,1/4]\) precisely when \(H\) is nondecreasing
on \([0,1/2]\); taking \(\phi=\widetilde H\) proves \eqref{eq:beta-order}.  If \(H\)
is strictly increasing and \(d_1<d_2\), then \(\widetilde H\) is strictly increasing
and \(g_{d_1}-g_{d_2}\) vanishes only at \(q^\ast\); hence the integrand in the
preceding display is positive off \(q^\ast\), and the inequality is strict.
\end{proof}

The beta representation and the likelihood-ratio order give one direction of the
reduction.  Concentration of beta laws gives the converse.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kernel-exact}
Fix \(m\ge2\) and symmetric nonnegative weights \(w_k=w_{m-k}\).  Then the
following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(G_{m,w}\) is nondecreasing on \([0,1/2]\);
\item \((u,v)\mapsto C_{m,w}(u,v)\) is Schur-concave on
\([0,\infty)^2\).
\end{enumerate}
If \(G_{m,w}\) is strictly increasing and the weights are not all zero, then at
each fixed \(s>0\),
\[
 d\longmapsto C_{m,w}\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right)
\]
is strictly decreasing on \([0,s/2]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that \(G_{m,w}\) is nondecreasing on \([0,1/2]\).  For
\(0\le d<s/2\), the fixed-sum representation \eqref{eq:fixed-sum} and \Cref{lem:beta-order} give the required monotonicity; if \(G_{m,w}\) is strictly
increasing, the inequality is strict.  At the endpoint, \(C_{m,w}(s,0)=0\)
by \eqref{eq:C-def}, while every interior value is nonnegative and is positive
when the weights are not all zero.  Symmetry completes the Schur-concavity
statement.

Conversely, suppose \(C_{m,w}\) is Schur-concave for every fixed sum.  Choose
\(1/2\le p_1<p_2<1\), and for \(s>0\) let
\[
 P_{s,i}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(sp_i,s(1-p_i)),
 \qquad i=1,2.
\]
The corresponding imbalances are \(d_i=s(p_i-1/2)\).  Since the prefactor in
\eqref{eq:C-beta} is common at fixed \(s\), Schur-concavity gives
\[
 \E G_{m,w}(P_{s,1})\ge \E G_{m,w}(P_{s,2}).
\]
But
\[
 \E P_{s,i}=p_i,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(P_{s,i})=\frac{p_i(1-p_i)}{s+1},
\]
so \(P_{s,i}\to p_i\) in probability as \(s\to\infty\).  Since \(G_{m,w}\) is a
bounded continuous polynomial on \([0,1]\), letting \(s\to\infty\) yields
\(G_{m,w}(p_1)\ge G_{m,w}(p_2)\).  Thus \(G_{m,w}\) is nonincreasing on
\([1/2,1)\), hence by continuity on \([1/2,1]\).  Symmetry makes it
nondecreasing on \([0,1/2]\).
\end{proof}

Kernel monotonicity is therefore precisely the analytic obstruction to
coefficientwise Schur-concavity at a fixed degree.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:fixed-total}
For the weights \(w_k=f_kf_{m-k}\) arising from \Cref{thm:main},
\[
 C_{m,w}(u,v)=[x^m]F_f(u;x)F_f(v;x).
\]
Assume \(f\not\equiv0\) and that \(F_f(u;x)\) and \(F_f(v;x)\) converge at
some \(x>0\), with \(u,v>0\).  Let \(N_u,N_v\) be independent with
\[
 \Pp_x(N_u=n)
 =\frac{f_n(u)_{3n}x^n}{(3n-1)!F_f(u;x)},
 \qquad n\ge1,
\]
and analogously for \(N_v\), and write \(I=\{n\ge1:f_n>0\}\).  If
\(m\notin I+I\), then \(C_{m,w}(u,v)=0\) and the degree-\(m\) sector
vanishes.  If \(m\in I+I\), then \(C_{m,w}(u,v)>0\), the conditioning event
has positive probability, and the power of \(x\) cancels under conditioning:
\[
 \Pp_x(N_u=k\mid N_u+N_v=m)
 =\frac{f_kf_{m-k}(u)_{3k}(v)_{3(m-k)}}
 {(3k-1)![3(m-k)-1]!\,C_{m,w}(u,v)}.
\]
Thus, degree by degree, \Cref{thm:main} is a microcanonical Schur-concavity
statement; summing these fixed-total inequalities against \(x^m\) yields the
ordinary pointwise Tur\'an inequality.  The beta-binomial identity
\eqref{eq:C-beta-binomial} makes these fixed-total sectors explicit, while the
fixed-sum form \eqref{eq:fixed-sum} compares parameter imbalance by stochastic
order.
\end{remark}

\section{The cubic residue kernel}\label{sec:kernel}

The exact reduction leaves one genuinely cubic question: whether the residue kernel
preserves central order.  For symmetric nonnegative weights \(w_k=w_{m-k}\), the kernel of
\eqref{eq:G-weighted} is
\[
 G_{m,w}(p)=\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}w_k\binom{3m-2}{3k-1}
 p^{3k}(1-p)^{3(m-k)},
 \qquad G_{m,w}(p)=G_{m,w}(1-p).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Weighted cubic residue monotonicity]\label{thm:kernel}
Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w-monotone}
 0\le w_1\le w_2\le\cdots\le w_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},
 \qquad w_k=w_{m-k}.
\end{equation}
Then \(G_{m,w}\) is nondecreasing on \([0,1/2]\).  If the weights are not all zero,
then \(G_{m,w}\) is strictly increasing on \([0,1/2]\).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The constant-weight kernel}\label{subsec:constant-weight-kernel}

Let \(w_k\equiv1\), and write \(G_m=G_{m,1}\).  Set \(n=3m-2\) and \(t=p/(1-p)\).
For \(0\le p\le1/2\) one has \(0\le t\le1\), and
\[
 G_m\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)
 =\frac{1}{(1+t)^{3m}}
 \sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\binom{n}{3k-1}t^{3k}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G-J}
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t}G_m\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)
 =\frac{3J_m(t)}{(1+t)^{3m+1}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-k}
 J_m(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\binom{n}{3k-1}t^{3k-1}
 \bigl(k-(m-k)t\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Bernstein-basis positivity certificate]\label{lem:bernstein}
For every \(m\ge2\), with \(n=3m-2\), write
\[
 J_m(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}b_j\binom{n+1}{j}t^j(1-t)^{n+1-j}
\]
in the degree-\((n+1)\) Bernstein basis.  Then \(b_j\ge0\) for every
\(0\le j\le n+1\), and
\[
 b_2=\frac{n-1}{n+1}>0.
\]
Consequently, \(J_m(t)>0\) for \(0<t<1\), and \(G_m\) is strictly increasing on
\([0,1/2]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(\nu=3k-1\) in \eqref{eq:J-k} gives
\[
 J_m(t)=\frac13
 \sum_{\substack{0\le\nu\le n\\\nu\equiv2\, (3)}}
 \binom{n}{\nu} t^\nu\bigl((\nu+1)-(n-\nu+1)t\bigr).
\]
To certify positivity on \([0,1]\), we compute the degree-\((n+1)\)
Bernstein coefficients of \(J_m\).  The projective substitution
\(t=\xi/(1+\xi)\) is chosen because
\[
 (1+\xi)^{n+1}t^j(1-t)^{n+1-j}=\xi^j;
\]
thus it converts the Bernstein basis into the ordinary monomial basis.
Introduce
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P-def}
 \mathcal P_m(\xi)
 =(1+\xi)^{n+1}J_m\!\left(\frac{\xi}{1+\xi}\right).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P-sum}
 \mathcal P_m(\xi)
 =\frac13
 \sum_{\nu\equiv2\, (3)}
 \binom{n}{\nu} \xi^\nu(1+\xi)^{n-\nu}
 \bigl((\nu+1)+(2\nu-n)\xi\bigr).
\end{equation}
For \(0\le j\le n+1\), coefficient extraction from \eqref{eq:P-sum} gives
\begin{align}
 [\xi^j]\mathcal P_m(\xi)
 &=\frac13
 \sum_{\substack{0\le\nu\le j\\\nu\equiv2\, (3)}}
 \binom{n}{\nu}
 \left[
 (\nu+1)\binom{n-\nu}{j-\nu}
 +(2\nu-n)\binom{n-\nu}{j-\nu-1}
 \right]\notag\\
 &=\frac{\binom{n+1}{j}}{3(n+1)}S_{n,j},
 \label{eq:P-coeff}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-def}
 S_{n,j}
 =\sum_{\substack{0\le\nu\le j\\\nu\equiv2\, (3)}}
 \binom{j}{\nu}(n-\nu+1)(2\nu+1-j).
\end{equation}
The second equality in \eqref{eq:P-coeff} follows from the elementary
identities
\[
 \binom{n}{\nu}\binom{n-\nu}{j-\nu}=\binom nj\binom{j}{\nu},
 \qquad
 \binom{n}{\nu}\binom{n-\nu}{j-\nu-1}
 =\binom{n+1}{j}\binom{j}{\nu}\frac{j-\nu}{n+1},
\]
the second of which is the first at \(j-1\), after
\(\binom n{j-1}=\binom{n+1}{j}\frac j{n+1}\) and
\(\binom{j-1}{\nu}=\binom{j}{\nu}\frac{j-\nu}{j}\).
For the coefficients \(b_j\) in the Bernstein expansion of
\Cref{lem:bernstein}, the defining property of the transform gives
\([\xi^j]\mathcal P_m(\xi)=b_j\binom{n+1}{j}\); hence \eqref{eq:P-coeff} yields
\[
 b_j=\frac{S_{n,j}}{3(n+1)}.
\]
Since every Bernstein basis function is nonnegative on \([0,1]\), nonnegative
\(b_j\) directly certify \(J_m(t)\ge0\) there; see
\cite{PowersReznick2000PositiveInterval} for background on positivity
representations on an interval.  It remains to prove that every \(S_{n,j}\) is
nonnegative.

Put \(\delta=n+1-j\).  A third-root-of-unity filter evaluates the sum over the
class \(\nu\equiv2\pmod3\).  With \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), one has
\(1+\omega=e^{i\pi/3}\), so the nontrivial Fourier terms have period \(6\) in
\(j\), and the closed form naturally splits into six residue classes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-table}
3S_{n,j}=
\begin{cases}
 \delta(2^j-3j-1)-3j(j-1),&j\equiv0\pmod6,\\
 \delta(2^j-2)-3j(j-1),&j\equiv1\pmod6,\\
 \delta(2^j+3j-1),&j\equiv2\pmod6,\\
 \delta(2^j+3j+1)+3j(j-1),&j\equiv3\pmod6,\\
 \delta(2^j+2)+3j(j-1),&j\equiv4\pmod6,\\
 \delta(2^j-3j+1),&j\equiv5\pmod6.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In detail, for
\[
 R_a(j)=\sum_{\nu\equiv a\, (3)}\binom{j}{\nu},
\]
the root-of-unity filter gives
\[
 R_a(j)=\frac13\sum_{\lambda=0}^2\omega^{-a\lambda}(1+\omega^\lambda)^j
 =\frac13\left(2^j+2\cos\frac{(j-2a)\pi}{3}\right),
\]
where \(2\cos((j-2a)\pi/3)\) equals \(2,1,-1,-2,-1,1\) according as
\(j-2a\equiv0,1,2,3,4,5\pmod6\).  The first two moments over the residue class are
\[
 \sum_{\nu\equiv2\, (3)}\nu\binom{j}{\nu}=jR_1(j-1),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\nu\equiv2\, (3)}\nu^2\binom{j}{\nu}
 =j(j-1)R_0(j-2)+jR_1(j-1).
\]
Expanding the summand in \eqref{eq:S-def} gives, for
\(j\ge2\),
\begin{align}
 S_{n,j}
 &=\delta\bigl(2jR_1(j-1)+(1-j)R_2(j)\bigr)\notag\\
 &\quad+j(j-1)\bigl(-2R_0(j-2)+3R_1(j-1)-R_2(j)\bigr),
 \label{eq:S-R}
\end{align}
with \(j=0,1\) following directly from \eqref{eq:S-def}.  Substituting the values
of \(R_2(j)\), \(R_1(j-1)\), and \(R_0(j-2)\) by residue \(j\bmod6\) yields
\eqref{eq:S-table}.

It remains only to inspect the six cases.  The cases
\(j\equiv2,3,4\pmod6\) are immediate.  If \(j\equiv5\pmod6\), then \(j\ge5\) and
\(2^j-3j+1>0\).
For the two remaining classes, note that \(n+1=3m-1\) is congruent to \(2\) or
\(5\) modulo \(6\); hence \(\delta\ge1\) when \(j\equiv1\pmod6\), and
\(\delta\ge2\) when \(j\equiv0\pmod6\) and \(j>0\).
If \(j\equiv1\pmod6\), the case \(j=1\) gives zero, while for
\(j\ge7\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-bound-1}
 2^j-2\ge3j(j-1).
\end{equation}
Finally, when \(j\equiv0\pmod6\) and \(j>0\), one has \(j\ge6\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-bound-0}
 2(2^j-3j-1)\ge3j(j-1).
\end{equation}
The inequalities \eqref{eq:exp-bound-1} and \eqref{eq:exp-bound-0} hold with
equality at \(j=7\) and \(j=6\), respectively.  Advancing \(j\) by one increases the
left-minus-right difference by \(2^j-6j\) in \eqref{eq:exp-bound-1} and by
\(2^{j+1}-6j-6\) in \eqref{eq:exp-bound-0}, both positive for \(j\ge6\); the
inequalities therefore persist for all larger \(j\).  Likewise \(2^j-3j+1>0\) at
\(j=5\), with increment \(2^j-3>0\) thereafter.  Hence \(S_{n,j}\ge0\) in every
case.  Therefore \(b_j\ge0\) for every \(j\).  At
\(j=2\), since \(\delta=n-1\), one has \(3S_{n,2}=9(n-1)\), and hence
\[
 b_2=\frac{n-1}{n+1}>0.
\]
Equivalently, all coefficients of \(\mathcal P_m\) are nonnegative and its
coefficient at \(\xi^2\) is positive.  Therefore \(\mathcal P_m(\xi)>0\) for
\(\xi>0\).  The substitution
\(t=\xi/(1+\xi)\) in \eqref{eq:P-def} proves \(J_m(t)>0\) for \(0<t<1\), and
\eqref{eq:G-J} completes the proof.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Insertion of monotone weights}\label{subsec:insertion-monotone-weights}

The constant-weight certificate is enough because the admissible weight cone is
generated by symmetric central windows.  The following summation-by-parts identity
characterizes the relevant dual cone.  Its one-sign-change form is the standard weighting
device used in this circle of coefficientwise Tur\'an inequalities; compare
\cite[Lem.~6]{KalmykovKarp2013Ratios} and
\cite[Lem.~2.1]{KalmykovKarp2013Reciprocal}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weighting}
Let \(A_1,\dots,A_L\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
 T_\sigma=\sum_{k=\sigma}^L A_k\qquad(1\le \sigma\le L),
\]
and let \(0\le w_1\le\cdots\le w_L\), with \(w_0=0\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abel-weight}
 \sum_{k=1}^L w_kA_k
 =\sum_{\sigma=1}^L(w_\sigma-w_{\sigma-1})T_\sigma.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^L w_kA_k\ge0
 \quad\text{for every }0\le w_1\le\cdots\le w_L
\]
if and only if \(T_\sigma\ge0\) for every \(\sigma\).  If all \(T_\sigma>0\), the weighted sum
is strictly positive for every nonzero such weight sequence.

In particular, if \(A_1,\dots,A_L\) has no more than one sign change, from
nonpositive to nonnegative, and \(\sum_kA_k\ge0\), then all \(T_\sigma\ge0\).
If moreover \(\sum_kA_k>0\) and \(A_L>0\), then all \(T_\sigma>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(A_k=T_k-T_{k+1}\), with \(T_{L+1}=0\), summation by parts gives
\eqref{eq:abel-weight}.  The cone equivalence follows because the increments
\(w_\sigma-w_{\sigma-1}\) are nonnegative, while choosing
\(w_k=\mathbf 1_{\{k\ge \sigma\}}\) recovers the individual tail \(T_\sigma\).

For the final assertion, suppose the signs change at most once from nonpositive
to nonnegative.  After the crossing, each \(T_\sigma\) is a sum of nonnegative
terms.  Before the crossing,
\[
 T_\sigma=\sum_{k=1}^L A_k-\sum_{k<\sigma}A_k
 \ge \sum_{k=1}^L A_k\ge0,
\]
because the omitted prefix is nonpositive.  If the total sum is positive and
\(A_L>0\), the same argument is strict before the crossing, while every tail
after it contains a positive term.  Hence all \(T_\sigma>0\).
\end{proof}

After pairing complementary residue blocks, a radial one-sign-change property shows
that every central window has positive derivative.  The duality lemma then inserts
arbitrary weights increasing toward the center.

For \(0<t<1\), differentiation of \eqref{eq:G-weighted} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-weighted}
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t}G_{m,w}\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)
 =\frac{3J_{m,w}(t)}{(1+t)^{3m+1}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jw-def}
 J_{m,w}(t)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}w_k\binom{3m-2}{3k-1}t^{3k-1}
 \bigl(k-(m-k)t\bigr).
\end{equation}
Pair the terms indexed by \(k\) and \(m-k\), as in the proof of
\cite[Thm.~2]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}.  For \(1\le k<m/2\), let
\begin{align}
 B_{m,k}(t)
 &=\binom{3m-2}{3k-1}
 \Bigl[t^{3k-1}\bigl(k-(m-k)t\bigr)\notag\\
 &\hspace{36mm}
 +t^{3(m-k)-1}\bigl((m-k)-kt\bigr)\Bigr].
 \label{eq:B-def}
\end{align}
If \(m\) is even, the central block is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-center}
 B_{m,m/2}(t)
 =\binom{3m-2}{3m/2-1}\frac m2\,t^{3m/2-1}(1-t)>0.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 J_{m,w}(t)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}w_kB_{m,k}(t),
\]
with the convention \eqref{eq:B-center} at the center.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:block-sign}
For each fixed \(m\ge2\) and \(0<t<1\), the sequence
\[
 B_{m,1}(t),B_{m,2}(t),\dots,B_{m,\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(t)
\]
has no more than one sign change, from negative to positive, and its final term
\(B_{m,\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(t)\) is positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(k<m/2\), the sign of \(B_{m,k}(t)\) is the sign of
\[
 H_{m,k}(t)
 =k-(m-k)t+t^{3(m-2k)}\bigl((m-k)-kt\bigr).
\]
The natural radial coordinate of the paired block is its distance
\(\eta=m-2k>0\) from the center.  Put \(t=e^{-z}\), \(z>0\).  Direct rearrangement
gives
\begin{align}
 H_{m,k}(t)
 &=\frac12(1+t)(1+t^{3\eta})
 \left[
 m\frac{1-t}{1+t}
 -\eta\frac{1-t^{3\eta}}{1+t^{3\eta}}
 \right]\notag\\
 &=\frac12(1+t)(1+t^{3\eta})
 \left[m\tanh\frac{z}{2}-\eta\tanh\frac{3\eta z}{2}\right].
 \label{eq:H-hyperbolic}
\end{align}
The function \(\eta\mapsto \eta\tanh(3\eta z/2)\) is strictly increasing for \(\eta>0\).
Thus moving outward from the center, which increases \(\eta\) and decreases \(k\),
can only decrease the bracket in \eqref{eq:H-hyperbolic}.  In particular, for
\(2\le k<m/2\), if \(B_{m,k}(t)\le0\), then the bracket is strictly negative
after replacing \(\eta\) by \(\eta+2\), equivalently \(k\) by \(k-1\).  Hence
\[
 B_{m,k}(t)\le0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B_{m,k-1}(t)<0
 \qquad(2\le k<m/2).
\]
The final block is always positive.  For even \(m\) this is \eqref{eq:B-center}.
For odd \(m\), the final block has \(\eta=1\), and its bracket in
\eqref{eq:H-hyperbolic} is \(m\tanh(z/2)-\tanh(3z/2)>0\),
because \(m\ge3\) and \(\tanh(3y)<3\tanh y\) for \(y>0\).  Hence the blocks have the
asserted sign pattern.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:kernel}]
Put \(L=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and define the central-window tails
\[
 T_{m,\sigma}(t)=\sum_{k=\sigma}^L B_{m,k}(t),
 \qquad 1\le \sigma\le L.
\]
By \Cref{lem:bernstein},
\[
 T_{m,1}(t)=J_m(t)>0
 \qquad(0<t<1).
\]
By \Cref{lem:block-sign}, the block sequence has at most one sign change from
negative to positive and \(B_{m,L}(t)>0\).  The strict one-sign-change
consequence of \Cref{lem:weighting} therefore gives
\[
 T_{m,\sigma}(t)>0
 \qquad(1\le \sigma\le L,\ 0<t<1).
\]
These tails are precisely the \(J\)-numerators corresponding to the extreme
central-window weights \(\mathbf1_{\{k\ge \sigma\}}\) in the paired indexing.

Now set \(w_0=0\).  The duality identity \eqref{eq:abel-weight} gives
\[
 J_{m,w}(t)
 =\sum_{\sigma=1}^L(w_\sigma-w_{\sigma-1})T_{m,\sigma}(t).
\]
Every increment is nonnegative by \eqref{eq:w-monotone}; hence
\(J_{m,w}(t)\ge0\), and it is strictly positive for \(0<t<1\) whenever the
weights are not all zero.  Equation \eqref{eq:J-weighted} proves the asserted
nonstrict and strict monotonicity of \(G_{m,w}\) on \([0,1/2]\).
\end{proof}

The step weight \(\mathbf1_{\{k\ge\sigma\}}\) retains exactly the central index
window \(\sigma\le k\le m-\sigma\); the cone of centrally nondecreasing symmetric
weights is the positive span of these windows, with coefficients
\(w_\sigma-w_{\sigma-1}\).  Thus the constant-weight certificate and the radial
one-sign-change property first prove positivity for every central truncation, and
monotone weighting is then only a cone decomposition.


\section{Proof of the cubic theorem}\label{sec:cubic-proof}

With the exact reduction and the cubic kernel theorem in place, the main result is an
immediate degreewise consequence.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:C-schur}
Fix \(m\ge2\), and let \((w_k)\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:w-monotone}.  Then \((u,v)\mapsto C_{m,w}(u,v)\) is Schur-concave on
\([0,\infty)^2\).  Equivalently, at fixed \(s>0\),
\[
 d\longmapsto C_{m,w}\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right)
\]
is nonincreasing for \(0\le d\le s/2\), and is strictly decreasing on this
interval if the weights are not all zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{thm:kernel,prop:kernel-exact}, including their strict parts.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:main}]
The zero sequence is trivial, so assume \(f\not\equiv0\).  The coefficients of
degrees \(0\) and \(1\) vanish.  Fix \(m\ge2\) and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w-from-f}
 w_k=f_kf_{m-k},\qquad 1\le k\le m-1.
\end{equation}
These weights are symmetric, and \Cref{lem:central-products} gives
\eqref{eq:w-monotone}.  Direct multiplication gives
\begin{align}
 [x^m]\mathcal S_f(s;d_1,d_2;x)
 &=C_{m,w}\left(\tfrac s2+d_1,\tfrac s2-d_1\right)\notag\\
 &\quad-C_{m,w}\left(\tfrac s2+d_2,\tfrac s2-d_2\right).
 \label{eq:delta-C}
\end{align}
This is nonnegative by \Cref{cor:C-schur}, which proves the fixed-sum
statement.  The equivalent Tur\'anian form follows from the parameter
correspondence after \Cref{thm:main}; the case \(s=0\) is trivial.
\end{proof}

The strict part of the kernel theorem also determines exactly where the coefficient
inequalities are nontrivial.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:strict}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:main}, put \(I=\{n\ge1:f_n>0\}\) and
\(I+I=\{i+j:i,j\in I\}\).  If \(s>0\) and \(0\le d_1<d_2\le s/2\), then
\[
 [x^m]\mathcal S_f(s;d_1,d_2;x)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\in I+I
 \qquad(m\ge2).
\]
Thus if \(I=\{a,\ldots,b\}\) the positive degrees are exactly
\(2a,\ldots,2b\), while if \(I=\{a,a+1,\ldots\}\) they are exactly
\(m\ge2a\).  The same description holds for \eqref{eq:Turan-def} whenever
\(\alpha,\beta>0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The condition \(m\in I+I\) is exactly that the weights \eqref{eq:w-from-f} are
not all zero.  If they all vanish, so does \eqref{eq:delta-C}; otherwise
strictness follows directly from \Cref{cor:C-schur}.  Since
\(I\) is an integer interval, so is \(I+I\).  The Tur\'anian statement follows
from the parameter equivalence and the fact that \(\alpha,\beta>0\) exactly
corresponds to \(d_1<d_2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:local-weight}
At a fixed degree \(m\), only the single chain
\[
 f_1f_{m-1}\le f_2f_{m-2}\le\cdots\le
 f_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}f_{\lceil m/2\rceil}
\]
is needed.  Thus the degree-\(m\) conclusions hold for any nonnegative sequence
satisfying that local condition.  Globally, however,
\Cref{lem:central-products} shows that log-concavity together with interval
support is not merely a convenient sufficient hypothesis: it is exactly the
condition that all of these anti-diagonal product chains are centrally
nondecreasing simultaneously.  Interval support also makes \(I+I\) an interval
in \Cref{cor:strict}.
\end{remark}

\section{The sharp multiplicity threshold}\label{sec:threshold}

The cubic theorem closes the only unresolved universal case.  Degree three already
explains why no larger multiplicity can satisfy the same statement.

\begin{proposition}[Degree-three multiplicity threshold]
\label{prop:multiplicity-threshold}
For every integer \(r\ge2\), the degree-three constant-weight kernel is
\[
 G^{(r)}_3(p)
 =\binom{3r-2}{r-1}[p(1-p)]^r\bigl(p^r+(1-p)^r\bigr),
\]
and, writing it as \(\widehat G^{(r)}_3(q)\) with \(q=p(1-p)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-central-slope}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d q}\widehat G^{(r)}_3(q)\right|_{q=1/4}
 =\binom{3r-2}{r-1}r(3-r)2^{2-3r}.
\end{equation}
For the two-term sequence \(f_1=f_2=1\), \(f_n=0\) for \(n\ge3\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-degree-three}
 \begin{split}
 &[x^3]\Bigl(
 F_{f,r}(\mu+1;x)^2-F_{f,r}(\mu;x)F_{f,r}(\mu+2;x)\Bigr)\\
 &\qquad=
 \frac{r\bigl(4r-(r-3)\mu\bigr)
 (\mu+2)_{r-2}(\mu+1)_{2r-1}}
 {(r-1)!(2r-1)!}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Hence for every \(r\ge4\) this coefficient is negative exactly when
\(\mu>4r/(r-3)\).  Moreover, this counterexample is minimal both in degree and
in positive-support size: degree two is nonnegative for every \(r\), and no
one-point-support sequence can produce a negative coefficient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For general multiplicity \(r\), the reduction
\eqref{eq:C-def}--\eqref{eq:G-weighted} runs with \(3\) replaced by \(r\)
throughout, giving the constant-weight kernel
\[
 G^{(r)}_m(p)=p(1-p)\Pp\{\operatorname{Bin}(rm-2,p)\equiv r-1\pmod r\}.
\]
At the first degree with a nontrivial complementary pair, \(m=3\), only the
residues corresponding to \(k=1,2\) occur, which gives the closed form for
\(G^{(r)}_3\) asserted in the proposition.  Put \(p=1/2+x\) and \(q=p(1-p)=1/4-x^2\).  Expansion at the
center gives
\[
 G^{(r)}_3\!\left(\frac12+x\right)
 =\binom{3r-2}{r-1}
  \left(2^{1-3r}+r(r-3)2^{2-3r}x^2+O(x^4)\right),
\]
which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:r-central-slope}.  Thus the center is a
quadratic maximum for \(r=2\), while for every \(r\ge4\) it is a quadratic
local minimum.  At the critical value \(r=3\) the quadratic term cancels, but
\[
 G^{(3)}_3(p)=21q^3(1-3q),
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d q}G^{(3)}_3=63q^2(1-4q)>0
 \quad(0<q<1/4),
\]
and
\[
 G^{(3)}_3\!\left(\frac12+x\right)
 =\frac{21}{256}-\frac{63}{8}x^4+O(x^6).
\]
Hence the degree-three cubic center is a quartically flat maximum: the first
local obstruction is exactly neutral at quadratic order, and \Cref{thm:kernel}
supplies the global positivity that this local calculation alone cannot see.

For the stated two-term sequence, direct multiplication gives
\begin{align*}
 &[x^3]\Bigl(
 F_{f,r}(\mu+1;x)^2-F_{f,r}(\mu;x)F_{f,r}(\mu+2;x)\Bigr)\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{2(\mu+1)_r(\mu+1)_{2r}
 -(\mu)_r(\mu+2)_{2r}
 -(\mu)_{2r}(\mu+2)_r}
 {(r-1)!(2r-1)!},
\end{align*}
Factoring out \((\mu+2)_{r-2}(\mu+1)_{2r-1}\) leaves
\(r\bigl(4r-(r-3)\mu\bigr)\), giving \eqref{eq:r-degree-three}.  The extracted
Pochhammer factors are positive for \(\mu\ge0\), so for \(r\ge4\) the sign
changes exactly at \(\mu=4r/(r-3)\).  The repeated factor \(r-3\) in
\eqref{eq:r-central-slope} and \eqref{eq:r-degree-three} reflects the same
mechanism: as \(\mu\) grows, the beta laws in the fixed-sum representation
concentrate near \(q=1/4\), so the finite-shift comparison increasingly probes
the local central behavior of the kernel.  At \(r=4\) the threshold is
\(\mu=16\), giving an exact counterpart to the numerical observation of
\cite[Rem.~5]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}.

Finally, for every integer \(N\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d^2}{\mathrm d\mu^2}\log(\mu)_N
 =-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{(\mu+j)^2}<0
 \qquad(\mu>0),
\]
so \(\mu\mapsto(\mu)_N\) is log-concave and its generalized Tur\'anian is
nonnegative (with the boundary \(\mu=0\) immediate).  The degree-two
coefficient is a nonnegative multiple of this Tur\'anian with \(N=r\).  If
\(f\) has one-point support \(\{a\}\), its only active product degree is
\(2a\), where the same argument applies with \(N=ra\).  Thus neither a lower
degree nor a smaller positive support can furnish a counterexample.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp multiplicity classification]\label{cor:multiplicity}
For integers \(r\ge2\), the family \(F_{f,r}\) in \eqref{eq:general-r} is
coefficientwise log-concave for every nonnegative log-concave sequence with no
internal zeros if and only if
\[
 r\in\{2,3\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The case \(r=2\) is \cite[Thm.~2]{KarpZhang2024LogConcavity}, and the case
\(r=3\) is \Cref{thm:main}.  For every \(r\ge4\), the two-term sequence in
\Cref{prop:multiplicity-threshold} satisfies all sequence hypotheses, while
\eqref{eq:r-degree-three} is negative for
\(\mu>4r/(r-3)\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The first supercritical case}\label{subsec:first-supercritical-case}
The Bernstein-basis positivity certificate of \Cref{lem:bernstein} can fail
even when the kernel remains monotone.  In the analogue
\((1+\xi)^{4m-1}J^{(4)}_m(\xi/(1+\xi))\), the coefficients at \(\xi^7\) and
\(\xi^8\) are
\begin{align*}
 a_7&=\tfrac{8}{315}(m-2)(m-1)(2m-3)(2m-1)(4m-7)(4m-5)(4m-3),\\
 a_8&=-\tfrac72a_7,
\end{align*}
and \(a_7>0\) precisely for \(m\ge3\), so the certificate fails there.
Nevertheless, at \(m=4\), with
\(z=(1-t)^2/t>0\),
\[
 J^{(4)}_4(t)
 =52t^7(1-t)\bigl(7z^4+42z^3+42(z-1)^2+6\bigr)>0
 \qquad(0<t<1).
\]
Thus failure of this Bernstein certificate is not equivalent to failure of kernel
monotonicity.  The first supercritical multiplicity therefore retains positive
structure beyond the universal theorem.



\section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

\subsection{Hypergeometric specialization}\label{subsec:hypergeometric-specialization}

For \(f_n\equiv1\), the triplication formula gives
\[
 F_f(\mu;x)
 =3x\,\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\,
 {}_3F_2\!\left(\tfrac{\mu}{3},\tfrac{\mu+1}{3},\tfrac{\mu+2}{3};\,
 \tfrac13,\tfrac23;\,x\right).
\]
Hence \Cref{thm:main} yields coefficientwise generalized Tur\'an inequalities for
this differentiated \({}_3F_2\) family, while general log-concave \((f_n)\) gives a
coefficient-weighted extension.  Related coefficientwise inequalities for generalized
hypergeometric functions under simultaneous parameter shifts are studied in
\cite[Thm.~3]{KalmykovKarp2017LogConcavity}.


\subsection{Strict pointwise log-concavity}\label{subsec:strict-pointwise-log-concavity}

The exact support of \Cref{cor:strict} upgrades the pointwise Tur\'an inequality
from nonnegativity to strictness.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ordinary}
Suppose \((f_n)\) is not identically zero, and let \(R\) be the radius of
convergence of \(\sum_{n\ge1}f_nx^n\).  For every \(0<x<R\), the function
\(\mu\mapsto F_f(\mu;x)\) is finite, continuous, and positive on \((0,\infty)\),
and \(\mu\mapsto\log F_f(\mu;x)\) is strictly concave there.  More generally,
for \(\mu\ge0\) and \(\alpha,\beta>0\),
\[
 F_f(\mu+\alpha;x)F_f(\mu+\beta;x)
 >F_f(\mu;x)F_f(\mu+\alpha+\beta;x).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Log-concavity with no internal zeros makes \(f_{n+1}/f_n\) nonincreasing on the
positive support.  If that support is finite, then \(R=\infty\).  Otherwise it
has the form \(\{a,a+1,\ldots\}\), and
\[
 f_n\le f_a\left(\frac{f_{a+1}}{f_a}\right)^{n-a}\qquad(n\ge a),
\]
so again \(R>0\).  For fixed \(\mu>0\),
\[
 \frac{(\mu)_{3n}}{(3n-1)!}
 =\frac{\Gamma(3n+\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu)\Gamma(3n)}
 \sim\frac{(3n)^\mu}{\Gamma(\mu)},
\]
so \(F_f(\mu;\cdot)\) has radius \(R\).

For local uniform convergence in \(\mu\), fix \(M>0\) and \(0<x<R\).  For
\(0\le\mu\le M\),
\[
 \frac{(\mu)_{3n}}{(3n-1)!}
 =\mu\prod_{j=1}^{3n-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{j}\right)
 \le M\exp\!\left(M\sum_{j=1}^{3n-1}\frac1j\right)
 \le Me^M(3n)^M.
\]
Choose \(y\) with \(x<y<R\).  Since \(n^M(x/y)^n\) is bounded, the summands of
\(F_f(\mu;x)\) are uniformly dominated for \(0\le\mu\le M\) by a constant
multiple of \(f_ny^n\).  The Weierstrass test gives local uniform convergence,
hence continuity; positivity is immediate from the coefficients.

Choose \(n_0\) with \(f_{n_0}>0\).  By \Cref{cor:strict}, the coefficient of
\(x^{2n_0}\) in the generalized Tur\'anian is positive for
\(\alpha,\beta>0\), while all coefficients are nonnegative by \Cref{thm:main}.
Summing gives the strict inequality.  Taking \(\alpha=\beta=h>0\) yields strict
midpoint concavity of \(\log F_f(\mu;x)\); continuity gives concavity, and equality
at any interior convex combination would force affinity on an interval,
contradicting strict midpoint concavity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Differential Tur\'an inequality}\label{subsec:differential-turan-inequality}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:differential}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:main}, for every \(\mu\ge0\) and every
\(m\ge2\),
\[
 [x^m]\left[
 \bigl(\partial_\mu F_f(\mu;x)\bigr)^2
 -F_f(\mu;x)\,\partial_\mu^2F_f(\mu;x)
 \right]>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m\in I+I,
\]
where \(I=\{n\ge1:f_n>0\}\).  In particular the differential Tur\'anian has
nonnegative coefficients, with the same exact positive support as the
finite-shift Tur\'anians of \Cref{cor:strict}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\ge2\) and use the weights \(w_k=f_kf_{m-k}\) from
\eqref{eq:w-from-f}.  If \(m\notin I+I\), all these weights vanish, so the
degree-\(m\) coefficient is zero.  Assume \(m\in I+I\).  By
\Cref{lem:central-products}, these weights satisfy \eqref{eq:w-monotone}, and
they are not all zero.

First let \(\mu>0\).  For these weights
\(C_{m,w}(u,v)=[x^m]F_f(u;x)F_f(v;x)\) by \Cref{rem:fixed-total}, so put
\[
 \Phi_m(d)=C_{m,w}(\mu+d,\mu-d),
 \qquad |d|<\mu.
\]
Differentiating the symmetric product at \(d=0\) gives
\[
 [x^m]\left[
 (\partial_\mu F_f)^2-F_f\,\partial_\mu^2F_f
 \right]
 =-\frac12\Phi_m''(0).
\]
The fixed-sum beta representation \eqref{eq:fixed-sum} remains valid for
\(|d|<\mu\), so
\[
 \Phi_m(d)=\kappa_{m,2\mu}\,
 \E\,\widehat G_{m,w}(Q_d).
\]
Use the density \(g_d\) from the proof of \Cref{lem:beta-order}.  Its formula is
even in \(d\) and has the form
\[
 g_d(q)=c(d)h(q)\cosh(d\ell(q)),
\]
with \(c(d)\) independent of \(q\).  Fix \(0<d_0<\mu\).  Uniformly for
\(|d|\le d_0\), \(g_d\) and its first two \(d\)-derivatives are
\(O\bigl(q^{\mu-1-d_0}\log^2(1/q)\bigr)\) as \(q\downarrow0\) and
\(O\bigl((1-4q)^{-1/2}\bigr)\) as \(q\uparrow1/4\), since
\(\ell(q)=\log(1/q)+O(q)\) at the first endpoint and
\(\ell(q)=O(\sqrt{1-4q})\) at the second.  Both bounds are integrable, so we may
differentiate twice under the integral at \(d=0\).  Using \(\int g_d=1\) gives
\[
 \left.\partial_d^2g_d(q)\right|_{d=0}
 =g_0(q)\left(
 \ell(q)^2-\E\,\ell(Q_0)^2
 \right).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Phi_m''(0)
 =\kappa_{m,2\mu}\,
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
 \widehat G_{m,w}(Q_0),\,\ell(Q_0)^2
 \right).
\]
Hence \Cref{thm:kernel} makes \(\widehat G_{m,w}\) strictly increasing on
\((0,1/4)\), whereas
\(\ell(q)^2\) is strictly decreasing there.  If \(Q_0'\) is an independent copy
of \(Q_0\), then
\[
 \begin{split}
 &2\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(
 \widehat G_{m,w}(Q_0),\ell(Q_0)^2
 \bigr)\\
 &\qquad=\E\bigl[
 \bigl(\widehat G_{m,w}(Q_0)-\widehat G_{m,w}(Q_0')\bigr)
 \bigl(\ell(Q_0)^2-\ell(Q_0')^2\bigr)
 \bigr].
 \end{split}
\]
The product is nonpositive and, since \(Q_0\) is nondegenerate, strictly negative
with positive probability.  Hence \(\Phi_m''(0)<0\), proving strict positivity.

Finally, at \(\mu=0\),
\[
 F_f(0;x)=0,\qquad
 \partial_\mu F_f(0;x)=\sum_{n\ge1}f_nx^n,
\]
because
\[
 \left.\partial_\mu
 \frac{(\mu)_{3n}}{(3n-1)!}\right|_{\mu=0}=1.
\]
Thus the differential Tur\'anian is
\((\sum_{n\ge1}f_nx^n)^2\), whose positive coefficient support is exactly
\(I+I\).
\end{proof}

\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

The cubic case is the endpoint of universal coefficientwise log-concavity for the
multiple-Pochhammer family.  Log-concavity of the input sequence is exactly central
increase of its anti-diagonal products; the beta mixture turns balance of the
parameters into stochastic centrality; and the cubic residue kernel preserves that
order.  These three levels fit together without loss: beta
concentration shows that the kernel is the exact fixed-degree obstruction.  At degree
three the central slope changes sign through the factor \(3-r\), so the same
mechanism also explains the sharp multiplicity threshold.

Beyond that threshold, universal positivity fails, but failure of the Bernstein
certificate does not force failure of kernel monotonicity.  \Cref{sec:threshold}
shows this already for \(r=4\).  Exact computation shows that, for
\(2\le m\le50\), the constant-weight kernel fails to be monotone only at \(m=3\);
we conjecture that degree three is its only failure.  Another natural question is: for
multiplicities beyond three, which restricted cones of centrally increasing symmetric
weights still produce monotone kernels?  Together these problems separate the
failure of a universal theorem from the finer positive structure that may survive in
the supercritical regime.
