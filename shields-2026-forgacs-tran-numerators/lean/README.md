# Lean 4 formalization — Fixed numerators in the Forgács–Tran family: bounded defect and angular zero discrepancy

Formalizes the core of `shields-2026-forgacs-tran-numerators.tex` against
Mathlib (`leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1`, namespace `ForgacsTran`).  `lake build`
is green with **no `sorry`** and **no project axiom**: every result below rests
on Lean's own `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` and nothing else.

For `1/(Q(t)+zt^r)` with `Q` real-rooted and positive, Forgács and Tran proved
that every sufficiently large coefficient polynomial is real-rooted, with its
zeros in an explicit interval [1].  Fixing a numerator `B` breaks that, and the
paper shows the failure is confined: the number of exceptional zeros is bounded
by a constant depending on `B` alone, uniformly in the index, and the zeros that
remain equidistribute in angle.  The bounded defect is the counterpart, in this
generating-function setting, of what is known for linear combinations of
orthogonal polynomials [2].

The proofs in the paper are noncomputational and do not depend on the code here.

## Result coverage

The theorems, propositions and corollaries of the manuscript.

**Work in progress.**  All proofs in the paper are unconditional and
noncomputational.  Anything noted below as not yet carried reflects the state of
the Lean formalization, not of the paper.

Every numbered result of the manuscript is formalized here, and the three clauses of
`thm:main` assume nothing at all: their binders are the pencil's own data.  The four
corners of the dominance grid cover \(\deg Q\ge2\), with \(\deg Q\ge3\) when \(r=1\);
`DominanceCellPartition.ft_dominance_cell_of_admissible` proves those four exhaust that
range, and `QuadraticDefect.ftCoeffPoly_quadratic_card_interior_ge` and
`quadReduced_card_outside_le` cover \((\deg Q,r)=(2,1)\) separately, by the
quasi-orthogonality route `rem:quadratic-case` prescribes and at every index rather than
eventually.  **One cell of the manuscript's hypothesis \(\max\{\deg Q,r\}>1\) is not
reached: \(\deg Q=1\) with \(r\ge2\).**  The branch geometry is available there
(`FTGeometryBoundary.ft_geometry_unbounded_at_branch_one`); the dominance and assembly
chains above it are stated from \(\deg Q\ge2\) and have not been run at a linear \(Q\).

`prop:angular-discrepancy` is formalized in both the whole-arc form and the form uniform in
\(0\le\alpha<\beta\le\pi/r\) that the proposition actually states.  It is produced at the general
admissible pencil by `AngularDiscrepancyAdmissible.ftAngularDiscrepancy_admissible` and its
three corner siblings, and `prop:equidistribution` and `cor:angular-rigidity` are composed
onto those producers at each of the four corners, so neither carries an analytic hypothesis:
their binders are the pencil's own data.

Two sub-statements of results marked covered are not carried, and are named here rather than
left to be inferred from a table.  `prop:equidistribution`'s second half — that the zero
counting measures converge weakly to the limiting distribution — has no formalization: the
tree defines no zero-counting measure.  And `thm:FT-geometry`'s opening assertion that
\(\tau\) and \(z\) are **real-analytic** on the viewing arc is not proved; what is proved
and used throughout is continuity, strict monotonicity, and a pointwise derivative.

That supply is produced in two halves, and both are closed at all four corners.
The dominance half is proved at each multiplicity of the smallest zero of \(Q\),
simple and repeated, and at each of \(r=1\) and \(r\ge2\).  The branch half — a
continuous phase on each block of the arc, its derivative bounded, and the total
variation across a family of blocks capped by one constant plus a multiple of the
numerator's degree — is produced by
`PhaseSupplyGeneral.ft_branchSupply_general_closed` and its three siblings, and
`ft_ftPhaseSupply_general`, `PhaseSupplyOne.ft_ftPhaseSupply_general_one`,
`PhaseSupplyRhoOneClosure.ft_ftPhaseSupply_general_rho_one` and
`ft_ftPhaseSupply_general_rho_one_one` assemble the whole supply from the
admissible class and \(B(0)\ne0\) alone.  At \(r=1\) the dominance half holds over
every numerator: `lem:amplitude-divisor`
gives \(p=\nu-(k-1)\) at a finite endpoint with \(k=2\) there, so the exponent is
\(\nu-1\) at every \(\nu\) and no condition on the numerator is carried.  That the
class really admits a numerator vanishing to higher order is exhibited rather than
asserted, by `WeightedDominanceBranchOne.ft_weighted_dominance_one_admits_double_zero`.
